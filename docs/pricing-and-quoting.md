## 1. Market benchmarks

Industry figures for wood carports, installed:

| Metric | Range | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Installed price | $15–$30/sq ft | Blended materials + labor |
| Materials only | $10–$20/sq ft (some sources: $3–$25) | Wide range because it depends heavily on roofing choice |
| Labor only | $3–$10/sq ft | Custom/skilled wood work trends toward the top of this range |
| Typical 12'×20' job, installed | $4,000–$9,000 | Our target starter job size |
| Larger 20'×20' job, installed | $12,000–$15,000+ | Premium materials/features push this higher |
| Industry-typical markup on materials | ~15% | Baseline to start from, not a ceiling |

These are **starting anchors**, not our prices. Real numbers come once we've run a few actual jobs
and know our true material and labor cost per square foot in this market.

## 2. The quote formula

```
Materials cost  =  (lumber + hardware + roofing + concrete) at our supplier pricing
Materials price =  Materials cost × (1 + markup%)      [start markup at 15–20%]

Labor price     =  Estimated labor hours × our labor rate

Permit fee      =  Pass-through, quoted as its own line item (see okc-metro-permit-guide.md)

Total quote     =  Materials price + Labor price + Permit fee
```

Keep materials markup and labor separate on paper even if the customer only sees one number — it's
the only way to see which lever to pull when a bid isn't landing (cut markup vs. cut hours vs.
change the design).

## 3. Size/style pricing tiers

Use this as the first pass on any inbound quote request before running the full worksheet:

| Job type | Footprint | Complexity | Starting quote range |
|---|---|---|---|
| Single-slope lean-to, attached | 12'×20' | Simplest — see `finding-cheapest-kits-simplest-jobs.md` | $4,000–$6,000 |
| Freestanding single-car | 12'×20' | +posts on both sides, more footings | $5,500–$8,000 |
| Freestanding double-car | 20'×20' | Larger span, bigger beams | $9,000–$14,000 |
| RV/boat length | 12'×30'+ | Longer spans, possibly taller clearance | Quote custom — span and height both drive cost non-linearly |

## 5. Where our price advantage comes from

The self-build vs. kit-retail gap identified in `finding-cheapest-kits-simplest-jobs.md` is the
core of our pricing strategy: a self-built wood lean-to runs roughly **$700–$1,000 in materials**
against a **$1,200–$3,500 comparable metal kit retail price**. That gap is real margin room —
we can:

- Quote competitively against kit-and-DIY sellers while still building in healthy markup, or
- Quote at kit-comparable prices and keep more margin than a company reselling manufactured kits

Either way, the pricing sheet only works if the materials-cost side stays accurate — revisit the
lumber/hardware/roofing numbers in §1 and §4 every few months, since lumber pricing moves.

## 6. Checklist

- [ ] Ran the job through the quick-quote worksheet, not a gut-feel number
- [ ] Kept materials markup and labor priced separately
- [ ] Checked the size/style tier table as a sanity check on the total
- [ ] Included the correct city's permit fee as a visible line item
- [ ] Set the deposit percentage per the contract template, not ad hoc per job
- [ ] Logged the actual final job cost afterward to tighten these numbers over time
