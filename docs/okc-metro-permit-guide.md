# OKC Metro Permit & Registration Guide

*City-by-city carport permit rules across the Oklahoma City metro — so we know, before we quote a
job, whether it needs a permit, a registration, or neither.*

The blunt truth about this trade: **permit rules are set city-by-city, not statewide**, and they
change. Treat every row below as a starting point to confirm with the actual building department
before quoting, not a final answer.

## 1. State-level baseline

- Oklahoma has **no statewide general contractor license** — general construction/carport work
  doesn't require a state-issued license from the Construction Industries Board (trade work like
  electrical/mechanical/plumbing does).
- **Call 811 (OKIE811)** is a state-law requirement before any digging, at least 2 business days
  ahead, and it's free. This applies everywhere in the state regardless of city permit rules.
- Frost line runs roughly 12" (south) to 18–24" (north) depending on county — footing depth
  requirements follow this, on top of the flat 12" code minimum below undisturbed grade.
- Design wind speeds across Oklahoma run 105–135 mph depending on location, which affects anchor
  hardware requirements for open-sided structures like carports.

## 3. Oklahoma City specifics

- A **standalone carport is not automatically swept into the residential building permit
  definition** the way an addition or new house would be — this is the closest thing to good news
  in this guide, but it doesn't mean "no permit ever," it means the trigger is different (often
  size- or zoning-based rather than automatic).
- If the carport is part of a larger residential project that *does* need a permit, carport square
  footage is counted in the fee calculation at **$0.25/sq ft**.
- **Contractor registration is the real gate**: to pull *any* permit in OKC, you register as a
  contractor first (~$100 fee). Do this once, early, regardless of whether the first job needs a
  permit — it's cheap insurance against a stalled job later.
- Source: [City of OKC — Contractor Registration](https://www.okc.gov/Infrastructure-Development/Business-Licensing/Contractor-Registration), [Permit Fees](https://www.okc.gov/Services/Permits/Building-Trade-Permits/Permit-Fees)

## 4. Norman specifics

Norman publishes an actual Carport FAQ, which makes it the clearest city in the metro to plan
around:

- **Permit required at 108 sq ft or larger** — a 12'×20' lean-to (240 sq ft) is well over this
  line; even most single-car carports will need a permit here.
- **Permit cost: ~$54.**
- Carports are classified as **accessory structures** — city setback rules for carports don't get
  a special exemption; standard zoning-district setbacks apply.
- Must be **open on all sides**, except the side attached to an existing structure (our lean-to
  design qualifies naturally).
- Approved materials: **metal, brick, or wood**. Cloth/fabric roofing is explicitly prohibited —
  rules out fabric carport covers as a legal option here.
- **Only one carport per dwelling unit.**
- Source: [City of Norman — Carport FAQ](https://www.normanok.gov/sites/default/files/documents/2025-04/250409_Carport%20FAQ's..docxFINAL.docxto%20website.pdf)

## 5. Moore, Edmond, and everywhere else

We don't have confirmed sq-ft thresholds or fees for Moore or Edmond yet. Moore requires permits
for "most residential improvements including sheds and accessory buildings" but the exact carport
threshold isn't published anywhere we could find — a phone call to Development Services settles it
in five minutes and should happen before the first quote in that city.

**Standing rule for any city not in this table:** treat it as "permit + registration probably
required" until a phone call proves otherwise. The cost of one call is a lot cheaper than a stop-work
order mid-job.

## 6. What this means for quoting

- **Build the permit call into the sales process**, not after the deposit clears. Confirm the
  jurisdiction's threshold and fee before finalizing a quote, and quote the permit fee as a
  pass-through line item, not absorbed into our price.
- **OKC contractor registration should happen in week one** of getting this business running (see
  the 90-day roadmap) — it's the one requirement that gates every future job in the city, so there's
  no reason to delay it.
- **Norman's 108 sq ft threshold is useful pricing intel**, not just a compliance fact: a carport
  just under that line is a genuinely simpler, faster, cheaper-to-quote job there. Worth knowing
  when a customer is flexible on size.

## 7. Checklist

- [ ] Confirmed which city/county the job site is in (don't assume metro-wide rules are uniform)
- [ ] Called or checked that city's building department for the current carport permit threshold
- [ ] Confirmed whether contractor registration is required in that city, and that we already have it
- [ ] Quoted the permit fee as a visible line item, not absorbed silently into the price
- [ ] Confirmed setback requirements against the site's actual zoning district
- [ ] Confirmed roofing material is allowed locally (e.g., Norman bans cloth/fabric roofs)
- [ ] Called 811 at least 2 business days before any digging, regardless of city permit status
