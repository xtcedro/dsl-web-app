# Jobsite Safety Checklist

*The non-negotiables for a small crew doing post-frame carport work — digging, ladders, and power
tools are where this trade actually gets people hurt.*

This isn't a substitute for OSHA's actual standards or a safety consultant once we have employees —
it's the floor, not the ceiling.

## 1. Before any digging — 811, every time

- **Call OKIE811 at least 2 business days before digging.** This is Oklahoma law, not a suggestion,
  and it's free.
- Applies to *any* ground disturbance — post holes for a carport absolutely qualify, even at
  shallow depths. Shallow holes still hit shallow-buried lines (irrigation, low-voltage, sometimes
  gas).
- A handheld metal detector/wire locator is a reasonable backup check right next to the house
  (where 811 marking is sometimes less precise), but it does not replace the 811 call. Skipping 811
  and relying on a detector instead shifts full liability onto us if something's hit.

## 2. Excavation & footings

Carport post holes are typically well under the depths where OSHA's full trenching/shoring rules
kick in, but the thresholds are worth knowing so we recognize if a job ever crosses them:

- **Trenches 5 feet or deeper** require a protective system (sloping, shoring, or a trench box) —
  not typical for a carport footing, but relevant if a job ever involves a deeper excavation.
- **Trenches/excavations 4 feet or deeper** require a safe means of egress (ladder, ramp) within 25
  feet of lateral travel for anyone working in it.
- Every excavation needs visual inspection by a "competent person" before anyone works in it — on a
  small crew, that's whoever is running the job, every time, not just the first time.

## 3. Ladder safety

The most commonly cited OSHA violations in construction are boring, repeated basics — which is
exactly why they're worth a checklist:

- Right ladder for the job — don't stretch a too-short ladder past its rated reach.
- Extend at least 3 feet above the landing/roof edge when climbing onto a structure.
- Set up on stable, level ground — never on loose dirt from a fresh footing pour.
- Inspect before every use: no broken rungs, no visibly bent rails.

## 4. Fall protection

- Falls are the single largest cause of construction deaths — part of OSHA's "Focus Four" hazards
  responsible for over 60% of construction fatalities.
- Roof work on a carport is lower-risk than a full building roof (shorter height, often single
  slope), but "lower risk" isn't "no risk" — don't get casual about it just because the structure is
  small.

## 5. Power tool basics

- **Gas auger**: two-person augers can kick back hard if the bit catches a root or rock — maintain
  a firm stance and know the kill switch location before starting.
- **Miter saw / circular saw**: standard blade-guard and hand-placement discipline; don't rush cuts
  on a job with a deadline.
- **Framing nailer**: sequential-trigger mode over bump-fire mode when working close to a second
  person — bump-fire is faster but removes a layer of accident protection.

## 6. PPE baseline

- Safety glasses — always, cutting or drilling of any kind.
- Gloves — handling PT lumber (chemical exposure) and metal roofing edges (sharp) both call for
  them.
- Hearing protection — gas auger and circular saw use both cross the threshold where it matters.
- Steel-toe boots — footing pours and post-setting both involve heavy, awkward loads near feet.

## 7. Why this matters beyond "the right thing to do"

- OSHA penalties in 2026 run up to **$16,131 per serious violation** and up to **$161,323 per
  willful/repeat violation** — real money for a small operation.
- Federal OSHA standards apply regardless of company size for most rules; some recordkeeping
  obligations phase in specifically once there are 10+ employees, but the safety standards
  themselves aren't a "too small to matter" exemption.
- Beyond fines: an injury on a two- or three-person crew isn't an abstraction, it's the business
  losing its labor capacity at the worst possible time.

## 8. Checklist

- [ ] 811 called at least 2 business days before digging on *every* job, no exceptions
- [ ] Confirmed no excavation on this job crosses the 4'/5' OSHA thresholds (flag it if it does)
- [ ] Ladder inspected before use, set on stable ground, extended 3' past the landing
- [ ] PPE (glasses, gloves, hearing protection, boots) on hand and actually worn, not just owned
- [ ] Auger operator briefed on kickback risk and kill-switch location before first use each day
- [ ] Nailer set to sequential-trigger mode when a second person is within range
