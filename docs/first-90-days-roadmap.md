# First 90 Days — Launch Roadmap

*How the other docs in this folder sequence into an actual launch, week by week. Adjust pace freely
— the point is the order, not the exact calendar.*

---

## Weeks 1–2 — Legal & financial foundation

- [ ] File Oklahoma LLC (~$100) — see business-formation notes
- [ ] Get an EIN, open a business bank account
- [ ] Apply for Oklahoma sales tax permit via OKTAP (~$20)
- [ ] Get general liability insurance quoted and bound *before* any jobsite work
- [ ] Register as a contractor with the City of OKC (~$100) — gates every future permit pull there;
      see `okc-metro-permit-guide.md` for other cities' requirements
- [ ] Read `jobsite-safety-checklist.md` together as a crew before any tools come out

## Weeks 2–3 — Supply lines & tools

- [ ] Open contractor accounts at a local lumberyard and a metal roofing supplier
- [ ] Confirm pricing on 26-gauge R-panel/5V-crimp in Galvalume and one painted option (see
      `finding-cheapest-kits-simplest-jobs.md` §3 for benchmarks to compare against)
- [ ] Buy core hand tools; line up auger and mixer *rental* rather than buying yet
- [ ] Secure a small storage unit for practice materials and offcuts — not a full shop lease yet
- [ ] Line up a truck rental arrangement (or confirm an existing truck covers early hauling needs)

## Weeks 3–4 — Design & pricing groundwork

- [ ] Run the reverse-engineering process (`reverse-engineering-prefab-kits.md`) on 2–3 target kits
- [ ] Settle on a first standard spec: post spacing, footing depth, default roofing package
- [ ] Fill out the pricing worksheet (`pricing-and-quoting.md`) with real local material costs
- [ ] Get the quote/contract template (`quote-contract-template.md`) reviewed by an attorney

## Weeks 4–6 — Practice & portfolio

- [ ] Build 1–2 practice structures (simplest lean-to design) using the standard spec
- [ ] Photograph every stage — this becomes the entire initial marketing portfolio
- [ ] Set up Google Business Profile as a service-area business; seed it with practice-build photos
- [ ] Create Nextdoor business page, join relevant local Facebook groups

## Weeks 6–8 — Soft launch

- [ ] Confirm the site's quote-request form is the single link every channel points back to
- [ ] Post first round of GBP updates and community-channel posts
- [ ] Start responding to inbound quote requests using the pricing worksheet and contract template
- [ ] Target the simplest job type first (single-slope attached lean-to) for the first paid jobs —
      lowest cost, lowest permit friction, fastest to complete and photograph

## Weeks 8–12 — First real jobs & iteration

- [ ] Complete first 2–3 paid jobs
- [ ] Request a review from every completed job, same-day
- [ ] Log actual material and labor costs per job — tighten the pricing sheet with real numbers
      instead of market benchmarks
- [ ] Re-evaluate: is rental equipment (auger, mixer, truck) still cheaper than owning at this
      volume, or has demand crossed the line where buying makes sense?
- [ ] Re-evaluate: is the storage unit still enough, or is it time for a real shop lease?

---

## The sequencing logic

Everything before week 4 is deliberately non-negotiable and low-cost: legal existence, insurance,
and the ability to pull a permit have to exist before a single job happens, and none of them require
guessing at demand. Everything from week 4 onward is designed to be reversible and cheap if demand
turns out slower than hoped — rent before buying, practice before marketing, market before scaling.
The only thing genuinely gated by real revenue is the shop lease and equipment ownership decisions
at the very end — don't front-load those costs before the first jobs prove the demand is there.
