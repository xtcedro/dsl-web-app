## 1. What every contract needs

At minimum, per standard home-improvement contract practice: project scope, materials, labor,
total cost, payment schedule, warranty terms, insurance disclosure, change-order process, and
timeline. Skipping any of these is where "he said, she said" disputes come from later.

## 2. Deposit terms

No Oklahoma statutory cap on home-improvement deposit percentage turned up in research — unlike
states such as California (10%/$1,000 cap) or Maryland (33% cap), Oklahoma doesn't appear to
regulate this by statute. That's not license to charge whatever — **industry norm is 30–50% down**
for a project this size, with the rest due on completion. Confirm with an attorney or our insurer
whether anything changes this once we're writing real contracts, and never treat "no state cap" as
"no ceiling that matters" — an unreasonable deposit is still a reputational and legal risk even
without a statutory limit.

## 3. Template

```
CONSTRUCTION AGREEMENT

Between: Definitive Structures LLC ("Contractor")
And:     [Customer name] ("Customer")
Date:    [date]
Site:    [job address]

1. SCOPE OF WORK
   Structure type:      [lean-to / freestanding / RV, etc.]
   Dimensions:            [___ ft × ___ ft, height]
   Materials:              [post size/species, roofing panel type/gauge/finish,
                            hardware system — reference the spec sheet used]
   Excludes:                [electrical, slab, walls — whatever is NOT included]

2. PRICE & PAYMENT SCHEDULE
   Total contract price:    $______
   Deposit (___% due at signing): $______
   Balance due:                    $______, due upon [substantial completion / final walkthrough]
   Permit fee (pass-through, see okc-metro-permit-guide.md): $______

3. PERMITS
   [ ] Contractor will pull the required permit(s) in [city]
   [ ] Customer will pull the required permit(s)
   Permit responsibility affects both price and liability — pick one and state it explicitly.

4. TIMELINE
   Estimated start date:      ______
   Estimated completion:       ______ (weather and material-lead-time contingent)

5. CHANGE ORDERS
   Any change to scope, materials, or price after signing requires a written change order signed
   by both parties before the change is performed. No verbal change orders.

6. WARRANTY
   Contractor warrants workmanship for [12–24] months from completion date against defects in
   installation. Manufacturer warranties on materials (roofing panels, hardware) are passed through
   to Customer per the manufacturer's terms; Contractor is not the warrantor of material defects.

7. INSURANCE
   Contractor carries general liability insurance of $______ . Certificate available on request.

8. RIGHT TO CANCEL
   See §4 — a 3-business-day cancellation right may apply for in-home sales; the required notice
   language must be attached if so (confirm applicability with counsel before first use).

9. SIGNATURES
   Contractor: ___________________  Date: ______
   Customer:   ___________________  Date: ______
```

## 4. The 3-day cancellation right

The FTC's "cooling-off rule" gives consumers a 3-business-day right to cancel for purchases over $25
made away from the seller's normal place of business (e.g., a kitchen-table sale at the customer's
home) — this is a federal rule, not state-specific, and commonly applies to home-improvement sales
made on-site. If quotes are regularly closed in the customer's home rather than at a fixed business
location, this notice likely needs to be included in the contract. Confirm applicability with an
attorney before relying on this summary — the exact triggers and required notice language matter and
change based on how the sale is actually conducted.

## 5. Checklist

- [ ] Attorney has reviewed the template before first customer use
- [ ] Deposit percentage set intentionally, not copied blindly from another state's cap
- [ ] Permit responsibility (contractor vs. customer) stated explicitly on every contract
- [ ] Warranty period decided and consistent across jobs, not negotiated ad hoc
- [ ] Insurance certificate on hand to attach or reference
- [ ] Confirmed whether the 3-day cancellation notice applies to how we actually sell (in-home vs.
      not) and included it if so
- [ ] Change-order process explained to the customer verbally at signing, not just buried in text
